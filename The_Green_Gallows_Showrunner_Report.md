# The Green Gallows — Season One
## Showrunner's Report

**Read for this report:** the Original Draft (Season Tracker, ~47k words), the published longread (rev. 3, "What You See"), and the Sisters Cut (rev. 3's spine with the original sisters' storyline). I ignored every note, purpose line, "why it works" and self-assessment inside the documents and judged only what would be on screen.

**How I treated the three documents:** the Sisters Cut is the current working outline, so most of this report is aimed at it. The other two are judged as source material: what each does better, and what the merge lost.

---

## 1. Verdict

**Not final-outline ready. Close on the spine, about one hard pass away on the sisters.**

- **Rev. 3's spine is the best thing in the project and is close to locked.** One prisoner, one chain, one price, one clock, one city. Nixon shooting Jamie and being assigned to find the shooter is a first-rate engine. Josephine is the best character you have.
- **The original draft's sister scenes are the best *writing* in the project.** The Birthday, The Surgeon, The Inspection, On Her Behalf, The Empty Box and The Stairwell are better than anything in rev. 3's version of the sisters.
- **The merge keeps those scenes "beat for beat", and that rule is the problem.** Many of them were written for a different mythology, timeline and Morgana. Transplanted intact, they now contradict the spine and each other. The Sisters Cut has roughly ten outright contradictions, an Episode 8 that holds two episodes of story, a revenge plot that idles for six episodes, and a finale gunshot that is not earned.
- **The original draft's plot should stay retired.** The Chapel, Virgil, the Penopticon, the march north, the shipyard, the train and the crawler are sprawl. Cutting them was correct.

The six structural problems in section 3 are the ones that stand between this and a final outline. Everything after that is cleanup.

---

## 2. What is working

Keep these exactly as they are.

**From rev. 3**
- **The countdown.** "49 Days to First Snow" and then snow arriving two weeks early at 0 DAYS. The audience's clock is wrong, and they find out when the characters do.
- **Nixon.** Shoots Jamie in the pilot, is handed the case in Episode 2, is leashed in 3, burns the log page in 4, seals the door in 5, confesses in 7, opens the folder in 8, offers himself in 10. Every episode moves him. "Let me be something on purpose" is the best line any man has in the season.
- **Josephine.** An assassin who married her target, poisons an inspector in open court for her son, sells her husband, pulls the lever, then walks into the White. "Then you know I love you" is the best scene ending in the outline.
- **Eldgard's death.** "I know. I checked. He means it as love." and "An order would have spoiled you." A villain confessing in a way that makes it worse.
- **Episode 5, "Lantern Night".** The strongest episode. Four plans converge, the mythology lands in one room, and the hero makes the wrong choice for a reason we understand.
- **The lunacy order.** A period-true trap with a period-true escape. It makes the wedding a rescue.
- **Small things:** "Toys after supper." "You're the son he actually wanted, Nix." The coffin full of slag. DIED ABROAD chiselled off the brass. "They'll hate me for a hundred years." "Us."

**From the original sisters**
- **The Birthday.** "It isn't real until it's mine." A forgotten birthday is a small, exact wound, and it pays off in the last scene of the season.
- **The Surgeon.** "I'm not wrong. I'm sorry. I'm not wrong." and "You said it wasn't real yet." The best scene in the project.
- **The Inspection.** She passes as Miriam by guessing. Alice sits on the floor.
- **The denial ladder.** Alice notices (Doorway), colludes (Inspection), destroys evidence (the paper in the stove), considers the knife (The Blade), lies to her employers (the report). Each step is an action, which is why six episodes of the audience being ahead of her can work.
- **On Her Behalf.** "If she'd asked, I'd have had to tell her it was already forgiven. And then she'd have had nothing left to carry." The scene is in the wrong episode (see 3.2), but do not touch the dialogue.
- **"Sit closer or leave."** The coat left on the landing. "Then he was unwell." The borrowed birthday.

**The theme is already there.** Everyone in this story stays warm on someone else's body: the Hardings on Morgana and the Hargraves, Tobias on his daughters, Eldgard on Nixon, Lambrick on Tamara, Josephine on Alice. The answer the season gives is one gesture, repeated: someone stands in front of someone else. Miriam's "collateral" is the Tithe at tenement scale. That rhyme is the reason the merge is worth doing, and no document says it out loud. Use it to settle every argument below.

## 3. The six structural problems

### 3.1 The revenge plot idles for six episodes

Edmund Lambrick appears in the Episode 1 tag and then has no scene until Episode 8, where he is named and killed inside one episode. The outline says the audience will "watch her not know it for weeks". They won't, because they will have forgotten him.

Alice does nothing on screen to find "a nobleman from the party" between Episodes 3 and 8. Her cover as "Miss Bounds, factory-safety assessor" is set up in The Terms and never used. Ajax tells her in Episode 4 that her sister is "meant to be in the river", which points straight at Lambrick's house, and she does nothing with it. Then strangers hand her a receipt. The protagonist's main want is solved by delivery.

**Fix**
- Edmund needs a touch in at least Episodes 2, 4 and 6. The original has usable ones: the mismatched cufflinks, the penknife at dinner.
- Give Alice a face-to-face scene with him, in cover, where he is charming and she likes him. That is the dramatic irony the outline promises.
- Make Ajax's line the lead she follows. She earns the name; the receipt only confirms it.
- **Suggestion (mine):** her first suspect should be Jamie Harding. A nobleman, from the Hardings' party, who died the night her sister jumped. It gives her a private reason to break into his room in Episode 2 and puts a knife under the romance with his brother.

### 3.2 Alice already knows by Episode 6, so the Episode 7 reveal reveals nothing

By the end of Episode 6 Alice has heard a surgeon say "engineering" and "exorcist", failed her sister on a memory test, been told "my memories feel borrowed", read a description of the Frost Killer, found her window blown outward, and been told by a nine-foot goddess "you smell of my daughter". In On Her Behalf the girl refers to Miriam in the third person and Alice answers in kind. Then they bury Miriam with the girl standing beside the box.

After that, "MIRIAM CAN'T DO THAT. You're not my sister" cannot play as a discovery.

The Empty Box in Episode 6 also breaks the Inspection: Alice risked everything in Episode 3 to convince the Vykra that Miriam is alive, and now holds a funeral for Miriam with Vykra agents in the back row.

**Fix**
- Move **On Her Behalf** and **The Empty Box** to after the reveal. On Her Behalf becomes a message delivered from the dead. The Empty Box becomes the real funeral, and it must be private.
- Move **The Bond** up into Episode 6 as the peak of denial: the apology Alice has waited for, accepted from the wrong person.
- Rebuild the reveal around rev. 3's idea, which the Cut threw away: **she knows because the apology was perfect, and Miriam could never have said it.** The Reflex and the green on the laundry floor then remove her last excuse. What is new in Episode 7 should be what the girl *is and has done*: the market, the dead, the thing the Hardings kept.
- This also fills a hole. As written, Alice goes from "stay out of my way" (Truce) to "I'm not losing another sister, not ever" (Promise) with nothing in between. On Her Behalf and the Empty Box are the missing bridge.

### 3.3 Episode 8 holds two episodes, and its clock does not work

Episode 8 contains Byron's death, the breakup over the letter, the wedding, the mutiny, the Orchard, Edmund's murder, the lost hour, and Morgana refusing the door. That is five climaxes. Episode 9's sister material is four short beats.

The order also breaks Alice. She learns from Josephine that the girl goes into the Heartglass *tonight*, and then goes to a club to hunt Edmund. One day earlier she swore she would not lose another sister.

Smaller clock problems in the same episode: Tamara is on a Gin Lane rooftop "on her wedding day". Six hours after her father tried to have her committed, she walks back into his street alone for a jewel case. Lambrick then speaks to her in The Tidy House as if the wedding never happened.

**Fix**
- End Episode 8 on the snow and 0 DAYS, with the girl taken and Alice holding a receipt with a name on it.
- Open Episode 9 with Tuesday, The Tidy House and The Lost Hour. Alice goes to Edmund first because she does not yet know where the Vykra took her sister. She wakes in the alley with snow on her and an hour gone from the one night when hours are all anyone has.
- Play that as her worst choice of the season: she spent an hour on the dead sister while the living one was in the cage. The title "I Owe Someone an Hour" then means something again.
- Rewrite The Tidy House so the strait-coat is in the room. Give Tamara a real reason to be there (her father's safe, which she robs in Episode 9 anyway).

### 3.4 The lost hour has no bargain behind it, and one substance does four jobs

In the original, Alice traded an hour to save Nathaniel. It was a choice with a price. In the Cut, Edmund breaks a decanter in her face. The season's cruellest device is now an accident.

Xyon is an abortion draught, an assassination poison that kills Felix in seconds, an amnesiac, and a compound tied to the green so that memory returns when the chain breaks. None of the last two properties is planted before Episode 8. If it kills Felix on the stand, it should have killed Miriam at the bridge and Alice in the townhouse. The name also sounds like science fiction next to Newgate and Gin Lane.

**Fix**
- Rename it and give it one rule: **the glass takes memory.** You already have the plant. In the Episode 8 cold open Eldgard swings a lantern in front of five-year-old Nathaniel and says "You had a dream." Move that earlier, or echo it earlier.
- When the chain falls, memory comes back across London, not only to Alice. Nathaniel gets the stairs and the lullaby back in the same minute Alice gets Edmund. The city was kept warm by forgetting. That makes the return a consequence of the theme.
- Separate the dose. The draught Miriam drank ended the pregnancy and did not kill her. What killed Felix was a different preparation or a killing dose. Say so once.
- **The hour must hold something the audience does not know.** They saw Edmund's guilt in the pilot. If the Episode 10 flash only confirms he poisoned her, it is a replay. See section 9 for my answer.

### 3.5 Tamara's shot is not earned

Tamara and Edmund share one line of dialogue in the Cut ("You look terrible"). In the source he is the half-brother promoted over her and a man she is building a case against. In the days before the shot, Alice told Nathaniel "Marry her" and saved Tamara from the asylum. An hour before the shot, Tamara left her own father to the wolves. Then she shoots Alice for Edmund.

The scene itself is good. "What did he say before you pulled the wire?" is a great question because it asks for the one thing Alice lost. The motive under it is missing.

**Fix**
- Give Tamara and Edmund three real scenes (Episodes 2, 4, 6). He should be the one person in that house who was ever on her side as a child, and she should hate what he became. Ambivalence is what makes a gun go off.
- Bring back "Ask first. Then decide." from the original. The shot should be about the answer, with self-hatred behind it: she has just let her father die and Alice is the only person left who can be made to account for anything.
- Decide whether the season needs two cliffhangers. Jamie standing in the White is the better hook, and it comes from the spine. Rev. 3's ending (the torn handkerchief, "Nine", the first time he says "Alice") is a complete ending that the Cut gives up for the shot. If you keep the shot, keep one grace note: **the first time Nathaniel says her first name should be over her body.**

### 3.6 Two Morganas, and scenes whose premise the merge removed

Rev. 3's Morgana is a chained mother who tears herself back onto the hooks to spare her child. The original's Morgana is a free predator hunting her daughter through London. The Cut keeps the first one's plot and the second one's dialogue.

- **Recognition on the bridge (Ep 7):** "Then I will take you by force", from a creature who, one episode later, chooses four hundred more years of hooks over harming her.
- **The Decoy Sacrifice (Ep 8):** the girl lights a flare to lead her mother away from Gin Lane. Her mother is chained under the Works and cannot go anywhere. The people actually chasing her are the Vykra, who employ Alice and know the address. The scene has no premise left.
- **The Choice (Ep 10):** "You could force me." "Yes." We have twice watched Morgana refuse to. There is no suspense, and the stakes have shrunk from London to custody.
- **Seam 1** says Eldgard has held the daughter for ten years as Morgana's leash. Episode 2 ends with Morgana learning "My daughter is here" as news.

**Fix**
- One Morgana: the prisoner. Her grief is the threat, and her freedom is the danger.
- Replace the Decoy with a surrender that has a reason. The girl learns what the cage is for (her, instead of the Harding boy Alice loves) and goes. That matches "I've come to take your place" and keeps DON'T FOLLOW ME.
- On the hill, the stakes are London. A freed goddess with four centuries of grievance, and her daughter standing between her and the city, "in the place nobody ever stood for Alice." That line is rev. 3's. Put it back.
- Either Morgana has known for ten years that Eldgard holds her child, or she has not. Pick, and fix the Episode 2 tag to match.

## 5. Mythology: seven rules that each need one sentence

The mythology is strong. These are the places where a sharp viewer will ask a question the outline cannot answer.

1. **"Willingly."** The Tithe must be given. Is that why shackled Orchard children stopped working, and why Eldgard had to raise Nixon to love the family? Say it.
2. **The Orchard's dates.** It "stopped walking in '39", yet Nixon's mother is on the wall with a date in the 1870s. What was it doing for forty years?
3. **Jamie or Nixon.** Eldgard prepared Jamie for the stair *and* bred Nixon for it. Which was the plan and which the spare? After Jamie dies, why does he say "Now it's you" to Nathaniel while the spare stands outside the glass?
4. **What Jamie's death paid for.** It "mended the crack" but did not pay the Tithe. Why not?
5. **The 1489 terms.** "Either way, my child goes free tonight." Eldgard recaptured her ten years ago. Did he break the bargain? If so, that should be why things are failing. Right now it has no consequence.
6. **What the Heartglass replaces.** The girl says "I've come to take your place" (she replaces Morgana). Josephine says she goes in "instead of my son" (she replaces the Tithe). Pick one.
7. **What brings the snow.** It reads as Morgana's grief. If so, she can call the deadline, which is worth knowing.

Also: the creature has too many powers. Frost, green telekinesis, the crystal-shattering scream, hearing the dead, total recall of the Harding drawing room, feeling Alice's pain across the city, and healing by freezing. Keep frost, crystal and the scream. Stage the laundry floor in that palette.

## 6. Characters

**Alice.** A strong lead with a weak engine. Her interior life is the best-written thing here. Her plot is passive (3.1), and the outline keeps her away from every supernatural event (the market, the Lantern Night scream, the wolves) so her denial can hold. She also chooses Nathaniel over her sister twice (the kitchen in Ep 8, "She stays" in Ep 9), and the outline treats the second one as tenderness. Decide whether that is a flaw the season knows about. One more: "she hates leaving the house" is said of a rooftop assassin. Give it one behaviour or it reads as a mistake.

**Miriam.** Under-defined at the centre. The Birthday shows a woman in love with good news. The Episode 4 cold open shows a hollow woman writing a suicide note that same day about "what I let him do". Those are two different stories. Was it love or coercion? Was the jump a decision or a collapse? There are also at least four letters (under the pillow, on the bridge, the unsent one in Episode 7, the scrap in the box). The note under the pillow would have been found by Alice in Episode 2. **One letter.** And answer this: Miriam is the principal and Alice the collateral. What do the Vykra do with collateral when the principal dies? That is a hard reason for Alice to keep "Miriam" alive on paper, and nobody asks it.

**The Lost One / Mira.** Excellent when she does not know what she is. **The Blackmail breaks her.** A girl who cannot pass a welfare check in Episode 3 runs a controlled extortion in Episode 4, with full memory of ten years in the Harding house. If she remembers that, she knows she is not Miriam, and "she doesn't lie, and she isn't sure" is false. Make it involuntary: the words come out in another voice, and then she is frightened of what she said. She also kills at least four people and nobody ever raises it again. She needs one beat of reckoning with someone she hurt. Cole is right there. On the name: "Something new" is a claim, and "Mira" is two-thirds of "Miriam". Own that in the line or choose another name.

**Nathaniel.** Rev. 3's version works: the useless son who chooses his father and builds a cage without knowing. In the Cut he has loose wires. He hears the crying come out of the girl in the Ward and drops it. He attends a funeral for Miriam next to a girl with Miriam's face and asks nothing. On the rooftop he says "You knew, you just couldn't afford to believe it" two scenes before he meets her. **Fix:** let him work it out early and say nothing, waiting for Alice to be ready. That is already his pattern ("I found the page under the others").

**Nixon.** Locked. Do not touch.

**Josephine.** Locked in rev. 3, blurred in the Cut. She now has two motives stapled together: destroy the girl as "evidence" (original) and use her to spare Nathaniel (rev. 3). Keep only the second. Also, she learns in Episode 4 exactly where the Frost Killer lives and stays silent for four episodes while the whole city hunts. Give the silence a reason and a trigger. In the Blackmail, a woman about to poison a policeman should not be frightened of an affair being exposed. She should be frightened because she recognises what is speaking.

**Eldgard.** Strong. See rules 3 and 5.

**Byron.** He dies for a girl he has never shared a scene with, and eight knives should not threaten a creature who shattered a market. **Suggestion (mine):** he was the one who fed the thing in the case, and she knows his step. And she has refused to use the cold since the market, so he fights to keep her from killing again. Then his death buys something.

**Tamara.** Good arc in rev. 3. See 3.5.

**Edmund.** A plot device with a penknife. See 3.1.

**Lambrick, Ajax.** Functional. Lambrick needs one scene of argument. Ajax's "your brother paid double" is a keeper.

**Tobias.** Two versions are still in play: the passive man who "took a fee" and the glass-sick vat-hand with the belt. Play both in the cold open: his hand shakes as he signs. That rhymes with Miriam's hand shaking when she signs five years later. His death report ("Good.", then she sits in the snow) currently lands on the core stair minutes before midnight in the finale. Move it. Also note that Alice never hunts him on screen, so "the confrontation she spent eleven years building" is told, not shown. Two robbed climaxes in a row is a risk; one of them has to be visibly wanted first.

**The Vykra.** Who runs it, Grise or Josephine? "Not why they were founded" is planted and never paid. Alice's duel with Grise in Episode 9 has no grievance behind it in this cut, because Grise never cut her out. **Suggestion (mine):** the Vykra were founded by Hargrave women after 1489 to break the chain. It would tie Nixon's story to Alice's and explain why a Vykra girl was once sent to kill Eldgard.

## 8. Continuity errors (quick fixes)

1. **"Months."** "I lost my sister months ago", "I mourned her for months", "even for a few months". The countdown says four weeks. She also "came back" the same night she died, so "I mourned her, then you came back" is from another draft.
2. **"She was twelve"** (On Her Behalf). Miriam signed at seventeen. Their father signed when she was twelve. "She watched them take you" also describes something that never happens.
3. **"The factory girl? That was years ago"** (Ep 10 flash). It was seven weeks ago, and Miriam was a Vykra blade.
4. **Did Miriam shield Alice or freeze?** The Doorway Test says she always stepped between Alice and any sudden sound. The Bond says "You didn't" and "I hated you for freezing". On Her Behalf says Alice believed she was left to it. One canon, stated once.
5. **Did Miriam tell Edmund?** The pilot says she dies "with the news still in her". The receipt says he bought the draught two hours before, so he knew. If she never reached him, how was she poisoned "hours ago"?
6. **The handkerchief.** It falls from Alice's glove in Episode 6. In this cut he never gave it to her, and since her knot is never cut, the place she keeps it means nothing.
7. **Alice's gauntlet** overloads in Episode 10 and is never built or seen before.
8. **"Fit for duty."** The Vykra pass Miriam in Episode 3. A blade passed fit would be given work. And the creature has "a shift at the laundry" by Episode 4.
9. **Miriam's "news" for their father.** Why would she seek out the man who sold them? Give her a reason (the indenture paper would be one) or cut it.
10. **The priest.** Alice "notes which priest goes in after her". Never paid. Make him the minister who reads the wrong name at the Empty Box, or cut the plant.
11. **"Old Vykra loyalists, not Josephine's people."** A faction that does not exist before it is needed.
12. **The engagement.** Nathaniel and Tamara are engaged in the pilot, so "Marry her" in Episode 7 asks for what was already scheduled. Have him decide to break it after the kiss, so the line costs something.
13. **Wolves.** Harding wolf crest, "wolf's eyes", wolves of the White. Connect them or thin them.
14. **Register.** Rev. 3 is 1889 London: Newgate, Bethlem, the Lord Chancellor. The preserved scenes bring "Xyon", "decommission", "Target Acquired", "What the fuck was that?" and "Oh, shoot — happy birthday, dear sister." That last line is the one false note in The Birthday.

## 9. Your open questions, answered

**What does Edmund say in the hour?** Neither of your two options. Both are about his method, which the audience already has. The hour should hold Miriam's *motive*: that the man, the child and the secrecy were her way to buy both sisters out, and that the last thing she asked him for was Alice's release. It answers the wound from the Surgeon scene (she had a life she kept from me) and from Episode 4 (she was going back to him). Alice gets it while bleeding in the snow, which is the cruelty you want. His tone should be banal. He thinks the wine was a kindness.

**Does Alice live?** Yes, and decide it in the room now. The open question for Season Two is what it cost: Mira is mortal, powerless and cold for the first time.

**Tamara.** See 3.5. Where she wakes up matters less than why she fired.

**Did Josephine know about the case?** She knew what it was. She did not know it could see. That is her shock in Episode 4, and it keeps her Episode 8 line ("a prisoner in that glass, Eldgard fed it") true.

## 10. Dialogue

**Lock:** The Job, The Birthday (minus "Oh, shoot"), The Surgeon, The Inspection, The Terms, On Her Behalf, The Empty Box, The Truce, The Tidy House, and everything quoted in section 2.

**Rewrite:**
- **"You're Not Her."** "I don't know what I am!" and "Don't you DARE pretend to be her" could be from any show.
- **The Promise.** "You made me want to be human. You made me human." It states the theme. Keep only "I'll be home before dawn." / "Promise?" / "Promise."
- **Recognition and the Decoy.** "They break what they cannot control." "That's what humans do." Wrong Morgana.
- **The Choice.** "I am something new. And I choose to stay."
- **Miriam's letter.** "The world failed both of us."

The pattern: your scenes are best when people talk about something small and specific (hair, a date, a rate, a seat) and worst when they talk about what they are.

## 12. Punch list, in order

1. Decide the facts of Miriam's last night: love or coercion, told or not told, decision or collapse, one letter.
2. Write one sentence each for the seven mythology rules.
3. Choose one Morgana and rewrite the bridge, the surrender and the hill to match.
4. Move On Her Behalf, the Empty Box and The Bond. Rebuild the reveal on the perfect apology.
5. Split Episode 8. Move the kill to the top of Episode 9.
6. Build Edmund: three touches, one scene opposite Alice, three with Tamara.
7. Restore the bridge bargain and hold it for the finale.
8. One memory rule, planted by Episode 4, paid city-wide in Episode 10.
9. Rework Tamara's motive, or cut the shot and end on rev. 3's ending.
