# The Green Gallows: Writers' Room
## Session notes on "The Sisters Cut"

*Question for the room: should the Sisters Cut, with the original draft's sisters storyline set into rev. 3's spine, replace the rev. 3 longread as the season? Does it make the story tighter and better, or only longer?*

Materials on the table: rev. 3 (the published longread, *The_Green_Gallows_Narrative.html*), the Season Tracker (*The_Green_Gallows_Original_Draft.md*), and the Sisters Cut (*The_Green_Gallows_Sisters_Cut.md*).

---

## Who's in the room

- **MARGO VESS, showrunner.** Fifty-one. Reading glasses pushed up into grey hair cut like a helmet, a man's cardigan with the elbows gone, a mug that says nothing. She has broken eleven seasons of television and trusts none of them. She runs the room from the end of the table with a red pencil she never uses.
- **DESH OKAFOR, story editor.** Thirty-eight, rolled shirtsleeves and a whiteboard marker behind each ear. He thinks in act breaks. He has already drawn the season as ten boxes on the board and put an X through two of them.
- **LENA PARK-WHITLOW, character lead, the sisters' writer.** Thirty-three, black turtleneck, chewed thumbnail, a paperback of the original draft so swollen with sticky notes it won't close. She has been waiting four drafts to get Miriam back.
- **RUFUS KAIN, genre and mechanics.** Forty-five, beard, Hawaiian shirt in November, a calculator watch he consults like an oracle. He cares about rules: what the frost does, what the dust costs, who knows what and when.
- **TOMMY BELL, the skeptic.** Twenty-nine, the room's rev. 3 loyalist. Hoodie, a vape he isn't allowed to use indoors and turns over in his fingers anyway. He wrote the orange, "Nine," and the perfect apology, and he is not letting them go quietly.
- **IZZY CALLOWAY, B-story: Tamara, Nixon, Lambrick.** Forty, tailored blazer, gold hoops, a legal pad with three columns headed TAMARA / NIXON / WHO PAYS. She's here to make sure the sisters don't eat her people alive.

---

## Round one: what actually changed

**DESH** *(tapping the board, tk-tk-tk)*: Let's be precise first. Rev. 3's sisters run on **grief and denial**. Miriam freezes on the bridge waiting for Jamie, the creature wakes in the morgue and says "Ali," and for six episodes Alice nurses a ghost with oranges and a counting game until a too-perfect apology gives it away. It's lovely. It's also **passive**. Alice reacts for the entire season. She has no goal of her own until she's breaking the chain in Ep 10.

The Sisters Cut gives her a **case**. A pregnant sister, a surgeon who says *"A nobleman. From the party,"* and an audience that has already watched Edmund Lambrick come upstairs with damp hands. From Ep 2 Alice is hunting someone. She takes Nathaniel's door because she needs the rooms where that man drinks. That isn't a tweak. It's an engine.

**MARGO**: Say the engine in one line.

**DESH**: Rev. 3: *a girl looks after a monster who's wearing her sister.* Sisters Cut: *a girl hunts the man who killed her sister while living with the thing that came back in her place, and she kills him and doesn't get to remember it.*

**MARGO**: The second one's a show.

---

## Round two: the case for

**LENA**: Three things, and then I'll shut up. Probably.

One. **The Birthday is the best pilot scene either draft has written.** "You've done your hair three times." "Twice." "Three." And then Miriam forgets the date, says *"Oh, shoot — I'm sorry. Happy birthday, dear sister,"* and walks out pregnant to a man who's going to poison her. The audience gets the whole relationship in ninety seconds, and the yearning, Alice wanting what Miriam has, is the wound the whole season presses on. Rev. 3's tunnel-and-orange opening is charming. This one hurts.

Two. **The Inspection.** "Ask me again in a fortnight and I'll be insufferable about it." The first time the thing is truly Miriam, it's impersonating her for a Vykra agent. Then Alice sits on the floor. Rev. 3 has nothing that does that much in one room.

Three. **"Already forgiven."** *"Because if she'd asked, I'd have had to tell her it was already forgiven. And then she'd have had nothing left to carry."* That's the thesis of the sisters in two lines, and it lives in Ep 6, where rev. 3 had Alice saying "After, Mim" and keeping the fence up. The Cut lets the abuse story out of the basement four episodes earlier, and it lets the Lost One ask on Miriam's behalf, which is braver and stranger than the perfect apology.

**RUFUS** *(squinting at the calculator watch)*: Mechanics, because nobody else will. The Cut **fixes the rules.** In rev. 3 the frost girl's cold costs her Miriam's memories, Wren lies about a heartbeat, the creature freezes a cat, the orangery hides her, Nathaniel meets her in Ep 3, and Morgana draws the White out of her in the finale. That's a lot of rules for one girl, and some of them fight each other.

The Cut has **one substance doing three jobs**: Xyon, gallowglass frost-dust cut into a compound. It's what Edmund bought for Miriam, what kills Felix on the stand, and what takes Alice's hour on the wedding night. When the chain falls and the green dies, the dust goes dead and the hour comes back. That's a setup and payoff tied straight into the season's main event. When the lights go out in London, Alice remembers killing a man. The finale's mechanics and the sisters' climax become **the same event.** You can't get much tighter than that.

And **the turtle.** The Lost One kept small in a glass case in Josephine's drawing room, the leash on Morgana. That one seam pays off Jamie's descent (he carries the case down), the blackmail (*"the day the latch was left undone"*), "You smell like the man who keeps my mother," and Alice's reveal in Ep 7. One prop, four payoffs.

**IZZY**: And from my side of the board, this is the bit I didn't expect to like. **Tamara finally pays for something.** In rev. 3 she's brilliant and right and safe: the cipher, the key, the vault, Nixon's hand on the roof at the end. In the Cut she has a dead brother she half-loved, a signet she took off his finger "before she'd decided anything," a lie she tells her father's face in The Tidy House, and then she walks out of the vault where she left Lambrick to the wolves and shoots Alice on the hill. And Nixon has to put the woman he loves face-down in the snow. That gives my whole column a Season Two. Rev. 3 tied everyone up in a bow.

**DESH**: Structurally, two moves I love. **Byron lives through the gasworks and dies in Ep 8.** That turns his death from a warm deathbed into a last stand with eight Vykra and a sentence he doesn't finish, *"Run. Find the boy. Tell him—"*, and it puts the reveal of Alice's contract into **a dead man's letter** instead of Josephine's receipt. The receipt was a plot delivery. The letter costs something. Then *"Was any of it real?"* over the body, on the morning of the wedding. That's your Ep 8 midpoint.

And **Kellow Ridge.** Eldgard signed the page that sent Tobias to the pits. The Lost One, in the cage, hears the Kellow Ridge dead arrive, and one of them says two names. *"Good."* Then Alice sits down in the snow. The Hardings made the man who made Alice. Rev. 3 never connected the sisters' abuse to the Works. The Cut does it with a signature on a roster.

## Round three: the case against

**TOMMY** *(turning the vape over and over)*: Okay. My turn. I'm going to be the bad guy because somebody has to be.

You're cutting **"Nine."** *"Nine is the one where we get up."* That's the best line in the series, and it pays off twice: in the knife fight with Grise and in the last scene, when Nathaniel says "Nine" and nobody told him. You're cutting **the perfect apology**, the reveal where Alice knows it isn't Miriam *because the apology is too good*. "Miriam couldn't have said it. Miriam flinched." That's a character reveal. What you've put in its place is *"Your eyes glowed green. You threw a man twenty feet. MIRIAM CAN'T DO THAT."* That's a plot reveal. It's louder and it's dumber.

And you've cut the bridge consent. In rev. 3 Miriam is still alive on the bridge and asks the girl to *"Go back to Ali. Stand in front of her. I never did."* "I said yes. Then I wanted to." That's the most beautiful thing in rev. 3's finale, and in the Cut Miriam jumps and something just pours in.

**LENA**: The Reflex is your apology, Tommy. On the laundry floor, before any green, she steps in front of Alice and takes the blade. Miriam's exact movement on a body that isn't hers. *Her sister came back for one second, and then she didn't.* The character reveal is still there. It comes one beat before the loud one.

**TOMMY**: It's there, but it's buried under a man flying twenty feet into a mangle.

**MARGO**: He's not wrong. Keep going.

**TOMMY**: Two. **Alice's denial strains.** Four people die at the crystal market in Ep 3 and she burns the news-sheet. Fine, that's denial and I'll buy it. But in Ep 5 she stands in the core and a nine-foot ice goddess turns to her and says *"You smell of my daughter,"* she comes home to find their window blown *outward* with her sister sitting in the glass with a nosebleed, and she still doesn't know until Ep 7? At some point the audience stops thinking "she can't bear it" and starts thinking "she's slow."

**DESH**: That's the one real structural risk, and the Cut half-answers it. On the rooftop in Ep 7 Nathaniel says *"You knew. You just couldn't afford to believe it."* So the design is that she knows from Ep 5 and won't say it. The page has to **play** that, not just claim it. After the blown-out window there needs to be one beat where we see her know: "Alice files it" is already there, and it's the right size. In the longread, the narrator has to make clear she's choosing it.

**TOMMY**: Three. **Nathaniel loses his Ep 3 turn.** In rev. 3 he tracks Alice into the lamp tunnels, takes the frost girl's bare hand, nothing happens, and he doesn't turn her in. It's the first time in his life he acts against his need to be useful. The Cut cuts that meeting, so he goes from "useful son" in Ep 3 to "chooses his father" in Ep 5 without the counterweight.

**IZZY**: He gets "The Terms" instead. He pays her rate without haggling and she calls him a tourist in his own grief. That's a turn, a smaller one, and it's a turn toward Alice rather than against his father. Honestly the Ep 5 choice lands harder if he hasn't already rebelled once.

**TOMMY**: Fine, I'll grant that one. Four, and this is the big one: **Ep 8 is a clown car.** Byron's Breakfast, The Promise, the false lead, Byron's Last Stand, the Decoy, the Wrecked Room, the piano, Nixon's folder, the chapel, the wedding, Target Acquired, the Peaceful Monster, Eldgard's arrest, forty Lanterns walking out, Nixon's wall of names, Tuesday, the Tidy House, the Lost Hour, the Heartglass, the snow. That's twenty beats. Rev. 3's Ep 8 had ten, and it already felt full.

**DESH** *(drawing a heavy box round Ep 8)*: He's right. It's the most crowded hour of the season. But look at what it is: the wedding is the spine, and everything else hangs off the same twenty-four hours. Dusk, night, dawn, noon, wedding night. It's *24*, not a clown car. If it needs relief, the fat is in the **Around them** stuff: Nixon's wall of Hargrave names can slide to the top of Ep 9 without losing anything, because Ep 9 already opens on his forty in the foundry.

**RUFUS**: Five, and this one's mine, not Tommy's. **The clock is wrong in four places.** The Cut inherited lines from the original, which ran over months. Rev. 3 runs on a 49-day countdown.
- Ep 7: *"I lost my sister months ago."* It's been four weeks.
- Ep 7: *"I mourned her. For months."* Same problem.
- Ep 8: *"Being your sister, even for a few months."* Five weeks.
- Ep 10 flash: Edmund, *"The factory girl? That was years ago—"* Miriam died forty-nine days earlier.

The first three are a find-and-replace. The fourth is a choice, and it's flagged open in the Cut's own notes. My vote is *"That was weeks ago—"*. It's colder: he's already filed her under "old business," and that's exactly why Alice answers "Not enough."

**MARGO**: Do it. Those aren't creative choices, they're errors.

**IZZY**: Last thing from me. Grise. Rev. 3 has Grise cut Alice's knot in Ep 4, and Alice folds the handkerchief into her glove where the knot used to be. The Cut keeps her on the contract until Byron's letter, so the knot stays, and the handkerchief just lives in her glove. It's a smaller image, but it means **Ep 9's knife fight with Grise is the first time Alice breaks with the Vykra**, and that's a better place for it. "You told me I had wolf's eyes." It pays off the Ep 4 flashback directly. Good trade.

## The roundhouse: final verdict

Margo goes round the table. One line each, then a vote.

**LENA PARK-WHITLOW:** "Rev. 3 gave the creature a sister. The Cut gives *Alice* one, a real one with a life she wasn't told about and a man who ended it. Every scene I fought for is back, and they're doing more work than they did in the original. **Yes.**"

**DESH OKAFOR:** "It's tighter where it counts. One case, one substance, one prop doing four jobs, and the finale's switch-off and the sisters' climax land in the same breath. It's heavier in Ep 8, and we can lighten that later. **Yes.**"

**RUFUS KAIN:** "Rev. 3's rules were vibes. The Cut's rules are a loaded gun on the mantel in Ep 1 that goes off in Ep 10. Fix the four clock errors and I'll sign it in blood. **Yes, with the clock fixes as a condition.**"

**IZZY CALLOWAY:** "I came in to protect Tamara and Nixon and I'm leaving with a better season for both of them. She shoots the woman who killed her brother on the morning she left her father to the wolves, and he holds her down. That's a Season Two. **Yes.**"

**TOMMY BELL:** "I'll say it plainly: the Cut is the better *story* and rev. 3 has the better *lines*. I'm losing 'Nine,' the perfect apology and the bridge consent, and I'll miss them every day. But Alice finally does something other than hold on, and I can't argue with that. **Yes, under protest.** Put 'Nine' in the drawer, not the bin."

**MARGO VESS** *(putting down the red pencil she never used)*: "Here's what I see. Rev. 3 is a beautiful season about a girl being kind to a ghost. The Sisters Cut is a season about a girl who wants something, gets it, and is robbed of it twice, first by her father dying in the dark before she could face him, then by a drug that takes the hour she killed a man in, and who only gets that hour back while she's bleeding out in the snow. The second season has a spine. The first has a mood.

The cost is real. We lose some of Tommy's best lines and Ep 8 is crowded. Neither of those is a reason to stay put."

---

# ✅ VERDICT: YES. GO AHEAD.

**Six votes to none, one under protest.**

### Applied in the new version (conditions of the vote)
1. **Clock fixes.** "I lost my sister months ago" → "weeks ago". "I mourned her. For months." → "For weeks." "even for a few months" → "even for a few weeks". Edmund: "That was years ago—" → **"That was weeks ago—"**.
2. **Alice's denial is played as a choice.** In the longread, the blown-out window in Ep 5 and the Rooftop in Ep 7 are written so the reader sees that she knew and wouldn't say it. No beats added; only how the existing ones read.

### Recommended for the next pass (*not* applied; your call)
- **Lighten Ep 8.** Slide Nixon's wall of Hargrave names to the top of Ep 9.
- **Keep "Nine" in the drawer.** If the counting game ever returns, it belongs to Season Two, between Alice and Mira.
- **The Ep 7 reveal.** Consider trimming Alice's "What the fuck was that?" run so the Reflex carries the scene and the green is the confirmation, not the discovery.

*Meeting adjourned. Somebody open a window.*
